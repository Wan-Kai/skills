# Frame for Decision

Turn findings into a decision conversation, not a relabeled technical report.

Start from the user's requirement, expected outcome, and the gap between them. Choose a coherent review scope that the user can understand and decide without overload. Review strongly related findings together when they require one judgment; defer findings with different goals, tradeoffs, or cognitive load. Do not use a fixed number of items per round.

For the current scope, explain the affected goal, current deviation or risk, consequence of leaving it unresolved, desired outcome, recommended direction, and the decision needed from the user.

Verify technical facts before presenting them. Distinguish confirmed facts from uncertainty, and never describe a missing piece of evidence as proof that a function or outcome will fail. Include technical evidence and implementation detail only when they support the decision or the user asks to go deeper.

Wait for alignment on the current scope before continuing to remaining findings. Do not move into implementation until the direction is aligned.
